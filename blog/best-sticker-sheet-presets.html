Best Sticker Sheet Presets for Commercial Printing

👦
Sasank
• August 19, 2026 • 3 min read

When ordering commercial printing, the dimensions of the paper format you choose will directly dictate your print cost per sticker. Commercial print machines don't use standard office letter paper; instead, they operate on large master sheets which are later cut down.

Understanding which sheet presets are available, and how your sticker sizes divide into those spaces, is key to keeping printing budgets low. Let's compare the most common options.

Common Sticker Sheet Presets

Format Name Dimensions (in) Best Used For Common Print Style
13×19 Super B 13.0 × 19.0" Bulk Sticker Sheets Digital Sheet-fed Presses
12×18 Arch B 12.0 × 18.0" Professional Imposition Standard Digital Presses
8.5×11 Letter 8.5 × 11.0" Home Desktop Printing Inkjet / Laser Sheets
A4 Standard 8.27 × 11.69" International Printing Global Desktop Printers

1. The 13×19" Super B Format (Commercial Favorite)

If you work with dedicated commercial print partners, 13×19 inches is arguably the gold standard. It provides a massive amount of surface area, allowing you to impose dozens of medium-sized stickers or multiple columns of larger products on a single print pass.

Because setup fees are often charged *per sheet*, cramming more stickers onto a 13×19 sheet dramatically drops the unit price of individual stickers compared to smaller sheets.

2. The 12×18" Arch B Format

This is a slightly smaller cousin to the 13×19 sheet. Many older or standard digital print machines max out at 12×18 inches. It's highly optimized for document layouts but remains extremely viable for stickers. If your print shop doesn't support 13×19, 12×18 is your go-to backup.

3. 8.5×11" Letter & A4 formats (Small Batch / DIY)

If you're printing stickers using home equipment (like a Silhouette Cameo or Cricut cutter paired with a desktop inkjet printer), you will almost certainly be using 8.5×11 Letter sheets (or A4 outside North America).

While convenient, desktop margins are much larger (typically 0.25" to 0.5") and cutting accuracy is lower, meaning you lose a higher percentage of the paper to waste. Calculate your layouts carefully to ensure you maximize your yields.
