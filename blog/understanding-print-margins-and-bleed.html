Understanding Bleed, Margins, and Gaps in Printing

👦
Sasank
• August 19, 2026 • 3 min read

If you've ever sent a design file to a commercial print shop only to be told it was "set up incorrectly," you've probably tangled with bleeds and margins. These three terms are the foundation of professional print preparation. Let's break them down simply.

What is "Bleed"?

When a cutting machine trims a sticker out of a sheet of paper, it is never 100% perfectly accurate. The paper might shift slightly under the rollers, or the cutting blade might wander by a fraction of a millimeter.

If your artwork ends exactly at the final cut line, that slight misalignment will result in a thin, ugly white sliver of unprinted paper on the edge of your finished sticker.

The Solution: You extend your artwork slightly past the cut line. This extra area is called the bleed. When the blade cuts, it cuts right through the printed color, ensuring a perfect edge. Standard bleed is usually 0.0625 inches (1.5mm) or 0.125 inches (3mm) on all sides.

What is a "Gap" or "Gutter"?

When you place multiple stickers on a single sheet, you can't always place them directly touching each other. The cutting blade needs physical space to travel between the stickers.

Additionally, because both adjacent stickers have "bleed" extending outward, putting them too close would mean their bleeds overlap, potentially ruining the prints if the cut is slightly off.

The Solution: The gap is the empty, unprinted space required between two stickers. Usually, 0.125 inches (3mm) is a safe gap for most plotters and die-cutters.

What is a "Margin"?

You might have noticed that your desktop printer can't print all the way to the very edge of a piece of paper. The same is true for most commercial digital presses.

Furthermore, digital cutting machines usually have rollers or registration marks near the edges of the paper that cannot be printed over or cut through.

The Solution: The margin is the designated "safe zone" around the entire outer perimeter of the sheet where no artwork or cutlines can be placed. A standard margin is often 0.2 to 0.25 inches (5-6mm).
